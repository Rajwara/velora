Driving the Autobahn: 8 Rules Every Visitor Should Know

12 Oct 2026 · 6 min read · By the Velora team

Germany's Autobahn is famous for sections with no general speed limit, but it is also one of the most disciplined road networks in the world. These are the rules we brief every Velora renter on before they leave.

1. "No limit" only applies where signs allow it

Large parts of the Autobahn have no general speed limit, but many sections do: around cities, roadworks, junctions and in bad weather. Digital overhead signs can change the limit at any time. Where no limit applies, the recommended speed (Richtgeschwindigkeit) is 130 km/h.

2. Keep right, always

The Rechtsfahrgebot is strict: use the right-hand lane unless you are overtaking, then move back as soon as it is safe. Sitting in the middle or left lane is not acceptable driving.

3. Never overtake on the right

Passing on the right is prohibited, with narrow exceptions in slow-moving, queuing traffic. Expect other drivers to assume you will not do it.

4. Check your mirrors early

Closing speeds can be very high. Before pulling out, look well behind you: a car that seems far away can arrive in seconds. Indicate clearly and commit to the manoeuvre.

5. Form an emergency corridor

As soon as traffic slows to walking pace, form a Rettungsgasse: vehicles in the left lane move left, everyone else moves right, leaving a gap for emergency services. Failing to do so carries heavy fines.

6. Keep your distance

A common rule of thumb is to keep half your speed in metres (at 120 km/h, at least 60 m). Tailgating is enforced with cameras and fines.

7. No stopping, no running out of fuel

Stopping on the Autobahn is only allowed in an emergency. Running out of fuel counts as avoidable, so plan your stops. Service areas (Raststätten) are well signposted.

8. Know your car before you go fast

Every Velora handover includes a walk-through of driving modes, brakes and assistance systems. Take time to get comfortable before exploring the car's performance, and only ever where it is legal and safe.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Rules and limits can change, so always follow the signs.
