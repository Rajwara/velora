Winter Tyres in Germany: What the Law Actually Says

Read the Guide All Articles

Germany has no fixed date for winter tyres. Instead, the law is situational, which confuses many drivers. Here is what it means in practice.

The rule: tyres must suit the conditions

In wintry conditions such as black ice, snow, slush or frost, you may only drive with tyres suitable for winter. Driving with summer tyres in these conditions can lead to a fine and points, and higher penalties if you obstruct or endanger others.

Which tyres count?

Tyres must carry the alpine symbol (a mountain with a snowflake, "3PMSF"). Older tyres marked only "M+S" were accepted during a transition period that has now ended. All-season tyres count if they carry the alpine symbol.

The "O to O" rule of thumb

Many Germans follow "von O bis O": fit winter tyres from Oktober to Ostern (Easter). It isn't law, but it is a sensible guide for southern Germany and the Alps.

Tread depth

The legal minimum is 1.6 mm, but experts recommend at least 4 mm for winter tyres to keep their grip on snow and slush.

What about rentals?

Every Velora rental is fitted with tyres that suit the season. If you are heading into the Alps, ask us about snow chains and Austrian or Swiss requirements.

Your own car

Our tyre service covers seasonal changes, balancing, alignment and climate-controlled storage for the set you're not using. We'll also remind you when it is time to switch.

General information only. Check current rules before travelling, especially across borders.
